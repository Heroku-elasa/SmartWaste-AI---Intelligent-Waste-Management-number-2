Game 1: competing for waste (prisoner's dilemma)
Two recyclers in one city can raise the buying price (compete) or hold it. Payoffs: annual profit (bn Toman).

One-shot Nash equilibrium: both raise prices (1,1) — bad for both.
In the repeated game, cooperation (6,6) is sustainable with a tit-for-tat strategy.
Our move: long-term municipal contract and our own collection stations to exit the price war.
Game 2: bargaining with the municipality (Nash bargaining)
Joint surplus: municipality's landfill savings + our profit from cheaper waste.
Our outside option: open market at 45k Toman; municipality's: costly landfill.
Nash outcome: equilibrium price ~30–35k Toman plus a profit/jobs share for the city — our 'target' scenario.
Strengthen position: offer jobs, landfill-reduction reporting and a 5% municipal stake.
Game 3: quality and trust with buyers (repeated game)
Short-term temptation: mixing cheap waste and lowering quality.
Buyers play grim trigger: one bad shipment ends the relationship.
At realistic discount rates, a loyal customer's present value exceeds the cheating gain 5× → consistent quality dominates.
Tools: lab certificate per batch, free samples, 6-month contracts.
Game 4: entry and deterrence (Stackelberg)
First mover in a city gains leader advantage: locks in waste contracts and customers.
Credible threat: expandable capacity (large plant) makes rival entry unprofitable.
Implication: rapid entry into the 10 priority cities after the pilot is proven.
Game 5: foreign partner and the hold-up problem
The Turkish partner is vulnerable after investing (sanctions, currency transfer).
Solution: staged investment (real option), in-kind contribution and value return via exported granule — a credible commitment.
FIPPA registration and international arbitration reduce risk.

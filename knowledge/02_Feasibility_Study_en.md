# Feasibility Study
Technical, market, financial, legal and environmental feasibility of the Mashhad pilot

1. Technical feasibility
Process: sorting → shredding → hot wash → drying → extruder with melt filter → pelletiser → bagging.
Iranian equipment (Pendar Polymer) is available; ~20-day delivery, ~25 workshops installed.
Needs: 1,500–2,000 m² land, 250–350 kW power, water and wastewater treatment.
Verdict: technically feasible. Main risk is summer power cuts; shift planning and a generator are needed.
2. Market feasibility
Supply: Mashhad has 3M+ people; plastic waste from scrap dealers, factories and the municipality.
Demand: pipe and bag makers in Khorasan; recycled granule is 20–40% cheaper than virgin.
Pilot output (~1,100 t/yr) is under 1% of the regional market.

4. Legal feasibility
Company registration, establishment permit (Ministry of Industry), environmental permit, operating licence — ~6–8 months.
Waste Management Law bylaw: Art. 17 maximum industrial facilities; Art. 12 recyclers' share of producer levies.
5. Environmental feasibility
Each tonne of recycled granule saves ~1.5–2 t CO2 versus virgin polymer.
Wash water must be treated and recirculated; sludge goes to sanitary landfill.
Conclusion
The project is feasible at pilot scale provided: waste contract at ≤40k Toman, purchase letters from ≥3 buyers, and stable power.
